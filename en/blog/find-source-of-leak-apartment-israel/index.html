Why is the stain not directly below the leak?

Water follows the easiest path: along a beam, a pipe, a duct or the top of a dropped ceiling. It can travel several meters before it marks the plaster. A stain in the hallway may start in the bathroom next door, or on a balcony one floor up. Binyan Bitoun begins by learning the stain’s history rather than opening anything.

Three questions save a lot of time. When did the stain appear? Does it grow after rain, or in dry weather too? Does it change when the AC runs or when the upstairs neighbour showers?

How can you tell if the leak is yours?

Start with the water meter. Turn off every tap, make sure no appliance is running, and watch your apartment’s meter. If it moves while nothing is on, water is going somewhere. The official water bill leaflet describes this same check. If the meter stays still, a leak on your supply pipes becomes less likely, and attention shifts elsewhere.

The test has limits. A leaking drain will not move the meter, since it loses water only while in use. Rainwater will not move it either. That is why Binyan Bitoun also checks drains, seals and outdoor surfaces.

How do you trace a leak, step by step?

With Binyan Bitoun, the search moves from simple checks to specific ones.

  1. Log what you see. Photograph the stain every day with the date, so you can see if it grows.
  2. Test the meter. Close everything and see if the meter moves.
  3. Isolate each use. Test the shower, basin, washing machine and AC one at a time.
  4. Talk to the neighbour. Ask for access upstairs, or involve the Vaad Bayit if common property is suspected.
  5. Share your findings. Send the photos to Binyan Bitoun on WhatsApp so the visit is well prepared.
  6. Treat the source. The cause is dealt with first, then ceilings and walls are repaired.

What if the leak comes from a neighbour in Israel?

Talk first, with dated photos in hand. According to Kol Zchut, the Israeli rights information site, each owner must carry out repairs in their apartment where neglect could harm another unit, and the Vaad Bayit must act on the roof and common areas. If nobody responds, the page says the affected owner can apply to the Land Registry Supervisor.

Binyan Bitoun can describe what is visible in your apartment and what was repaired, but it does not decide who is liable. That is for the owners, their insurers and, where needed, a lawyer. The guide on water damage and who pays walks through the scenarios.

What should you avoid while searching for a leak?

Do not repaint

Paint on a damp ceiling hides the clue you need, and stains come back through it. Binyan Bitoun repaints only when the surface is dry.

Do not open walls at random

Breaking tile or plaster without a lead is costly, especially in a bathroom. Run the simple tests first.

Do not wait it out

The US Environmental Protection Agency advises drying wet areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. A slow leak makes that impossible.

Do not forget the bill

According to Kol Zchut, a repaired leak can qualify for a reduced rate from the municipal water corporation, subject to conditions and within 6 months of the end of the billing period. Keep proof of the repair.
