Why does Givatayim have its own renovation challenges?

Givatayim is small and tightly packed. Wikipedia gives about 3.2 square kilometers for some 58,600 residents in 2024, and explains that the name means "two hills", Borochov and Kozlovsky. It also describes Borochov as the country’s first workers’ neighbourhood. Many buildings have just a few floors and date back decades. That makes for a charming stock of housing, tired in places. Binyan Bitoun sees three recurring issues.

The shared roof

A small building often shares one flat roof. When its waterproofing ages, the top floor gets the stains. The issue then concerns several neighbours and has to go through the Vaad Bayit.

Original pipes

In the oldest blocks, pipes and drains may go back to the original construction. A slow leak marks the paint first, then the plaster.

Narrow wet rooms

Bathrooms from that era are small and often badly arranged. A floating vanity or built-in storage already changes a lot. Walk-ups without elevators add another factor, since materials go up and rubble comes down by hand, so raise this at the quote stage.

How do you fix a top-floor stain in Givatayim, step by step?

The top floor is the classic case here. This is Binyan Bitoun’s approach.

  1. Watch the stain after rain. See whether it spreads, and photograph it with the date.
  2. Speak to the Vaad Bayit. Since the roof is often shared, the committee should know from the start.
  3. Have someone go up. The coating, edges and drains need checking in person.
  4. Treat the roof first. Waterproofing is restored before anyone touches your ceiling.
  5. Wait for the ceiling to dry. Damp plaster will not hold paint.
  6. Rebuild the ceiling. The surface is repaired, then repainted in a single even tone.

Who pays when a shared roof leaks in Givatayim?

The question comes up in almost every small building. Kol Zchut, the Israeli rights information site, says the Vaad Bayit must repair common areas where neglect could harm an apartment. Cost sharing then depends on the building: the page on Vaad Bayit payments explains that payments normally follow each apartment’s floor area, unless otherwise agreed. Binyan Bitoun can price the roof for the building, but check your bylaws and speak to a legal professional if a dispute drags on.

How do you gain space in an older Givatayim apartment?

Floor areas are often modest, so every corner counts, and a family that stays for years feels each lost shelf. A hallway can hold a floor-to-ceiling wardrobe. A niche becomes a laundry area, and an ugly duct vanishes behind boxing. In the bathroom, a floating vanity frees the floor and feels lighter. Binyan Bitoun enjoys this kind of job, and custom fitting is one of its services.

Lighter colors and a single flooring throughout also help a small apartment feel larger. Before knocking through a partition, think about structure. In an older building, have an engineer check the wall, because a load-bearing wall needs qualified advice whatever the design appeal.

Should you inspect an apartment before buying in Givatayim?

Yes, ideally with a technical eye. Look at top-floor ceilings, the bases of walls and the bathrooms. If you are abroad, Binyan Bitoun can visit, send you a video of the condition and propose a quote, before or after you sign.
